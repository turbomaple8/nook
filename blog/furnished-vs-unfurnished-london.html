Furnished vs Unfurnished Rooms in London: What You Actually Save

That £15/week saving costs £600 at IKEA. The honest maths.

Unfurnished rooms in London typically list £10–£20/week cheaper than furnished equivalents, and that gap convinces a lot of newcomers to "save money" by furnishing themselves. Sometimes that's right. Usually, for the people reading this, it isn't. Here's the actual arithmetic.

What Furnishing a Room Really Costs

The minimum viable London bedroom, priced honestly at 2026 flat-pack rates: bed frame and mattress £280–£450 (the item not worth cheaping out on), wardrobe £90–£180, desk and chair £110–£200, curtains, lamp, mirror and the fifty small things £80–£150. Realistic total: £560–£980. Add delivery slots, assembly evenings, and — the bit everyone forgets — a van or disposal cost when you leave, because second-hand furniture in London resells for pennies.

The Break-Even Table

Say the unfurnished room saves you £15/week. Against a middling £700 furnishing spend:

6-month stay: saving £390, spending £700 — you're £310 down, plus the disposal problem.
12 months: saving £780 vs £700 — roughly break-even, for a year of owning a wardrobe.
24 months+: saving £1,560 vs £700 — now unfurnished genuinely wins, if you're sure you'll stay.

The rule of thumb: under 18 months, furnished wins. Students, interns, placement years and first-year-in-London professionals are almost always under 18 months in one room — which is why the "cheaper" unfurnished route quietly costs them money.

The Costs That Don't Fit in a Table

Your first London week has enough jobs already: bank account, SIM, registering with a GP, learning which Tube exit isn't a mistake. Adding "build a bedroom" to that week has a real cost even if it never hits a spreadsheet. There's also the moving-out tax: every stick of furniture you own makes the next move harder and more expensive — flexibility is worth something in a city where plans change.

When Unfurnished Genuinely Makes Sense

Settling for 2+ years in one place; moving as a couple consolidating two rooms of stuff; or inheriting furniture free from a departing tenant. If that's you, negotiate: unfurnished landlords often accept £5–£10/week below asking in autumn, when the September rush fades.
