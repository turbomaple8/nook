A Masters in London: The Living Costs Beyond Tuition

Tuition is the number you know. Here's the other £17,000.

Masters applicants plan around one number — tuition — and then meet the second number at the visa stage: a year of London living costs. For financial planning (and for the UKVI maintenance requirement), you need the real figure, not the university brochure's optimistic one. Here it is, line by line, for a typical 12-month masters.

The Worked Annual Budget

Housing: £11,200–£16,000. The dominant line. An all-inclusive room at £215–£300/week over 52 weeks — and note it's 52, not the 39 undergrads count: a masters runs through the dissertation summer, which is exactly why a 12-month term fits the course like a glove rather than leaving you scrambling for a summer sublet. Halls and PBSA for postgrads run higher (£300–£450/week). The full comparison lives in the student housing cost breakdown.

Food: £2,600–£3,600. £50–£70/week cooking most meals, with a social margin. Shared kitchens genuinely cut this — batch cooking with flatmates is the postgrad's quiet superpower.

Transport: £1,500–£2,100. Zones 1-2 with the student railcard discount applied to off-peak. Live walking distance to campus and this line halves — the maths in the zones guide.

The lines people forget: phone (£120–£200), toiletries/household (£300), course materials and printing (£150–£400 by subject), winter clothing if you're arriving from somewhere warm (£200, non-negotiable by November), and the social life that makes the year worth living (£1,000–£2,000, honestly budgeted).

Realistic annual total: £17,000–£24,000 beyond tuition — the span between a disciplined Zone 2 east all-inclusive setup and a central PBSA studio lifestyle. The visa maintenance requirement (£1,483/month for 9 months in London at current rules) sits inside that range; budget to the real number, not the minimum.

Where the Budget Is Actually Won

Not on coffee. The three structural choices: housing format (all-inclusive room vs PBSA studio is a £5,000+/year gap — and bundled bills remove the winter energy surprise that wrecks Q1 budgets, per the winter bills guide); postcode (Zone 2 east vs Zone 1 is £2,000–£4,000/year for a 15-minute commute difference); and council tax, which full-time students escape entirely with ten minutes of paperwork — or never think about at all in an all-inclusive room (how the exemption works).

Funding Reality Check

UK postgrad loans don't cover London living costs in full — assume a gap funded by savings, family or part-time work (student visas typically allow 20 hrs/week in term). Plan the gap before arrival; mid-year money stress is the top reason masters students report regretting their housing choice, usually because they signed the expensive option in a hurry.

Applying for 2027 — September or January intake? Lock the budget now, choose the housing format second, pick the room last. The student housing guide walks the format choice, and Nook's all-inclusive rooms put the biggest line of this budget into one known number — from £215/week, subject to availability, 12-month terms that match the course.
