Condensation and Mould in London Rentals: Fixes and Your Rights

It starts with misted windows in November. Here's how to stop it owning your winter.

Every November, London's older housing stock starts its dampest party trick: misted windows by morning, a musty note in the wardrobe, and — left alone — black mould creeping along the window frame by January. It's the most common winter complaint in the city's rentals, it's half physics and half building condition, and the split matters because it decides whose job the fix is.

Why London Rooms Do This

People produce moisture — breathing, showering, cooking, drying laundry adds litres of water vapour to indoor air daily. Warm air holds it; cold surfaces collect it. A single-glazed Victorian window in January is a condensation machine, and where moisture sits, mould follows within weeks. Shared flats amplify it: more people, more showers, more pasta steam, same old windows.

The Tenant Half: Habits That Actually Work

Ventilate at the moisture source — bathroom fan on (or window cracked) during and 15 minutes after showers; lids on pans; kitchen door closed while cooking. Don't dry laundry on radiators — the single biggest hidden contributor; a £15 airer near an open window or in a ventilated room beats soaking your walls. Keep air moving behind furniture — a wardrobe flat against a cold external wall builds its own micro-climate; two inches of gap prevents it. Heat consistently rather than in blasts — steady background warmth keeps surfaces above the dew point; the winter bills guide covers the cost side of that trade. Wipe visible condensation off frames in the morning — thirty unglamorous seconds that denies mould its water supply.

The Landlord Half: What the Law Says

Since the Homes (Fitness for Human Habitation) Act and the Awaab's Law reforms, damp and mould stopped being a "lifestyle" dismissal: landlords must investigate and fix hazards arising from the building — failed sealant, bridged damp-proof courses, broken extractor fans, uninsulated cold spots, leaking gutters soaking a wall. If mould keeps returning despite reasonable habits, the building is the suspect, and that's squarely the landlord's duty.

The escalation ladder: report in writing with photos (dated — your evidence trail matters, same discipline as deposit protection); allow reasonable time for inspection and repair; chase in writing if ignored; then escalate to your borough's environmental health team, who can inspect and compel repairs. Never withhold rent as a protest — it hands the leverage back. And mould in a room you're viewing now? That's a walking-away signal per the viewing checklist, not a negotiating chip.

The Managed-Housing Difference

In a managed room the split disappears: one repairs channel, an operator whose business depends on habitable rooms, and no landlord-vs-tenant blame tennis. Mid-tenancy and fighting the November mist? Start the habits this week and the paper trail today. Choosing where to live next? Ask every viewing one question: "how does this room do in winter?" — and watch whether the answer comes with evidence. Nook's rooms, winter included.
