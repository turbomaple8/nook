Relocating to London for a Job: The Complete Checklist

One offer letter, one suitcase, one checklist — in the order that actually works.

Relocating for a job is a dependency puzzle: the bank wants an address, some landlords want a UK bank account, the NI number wants you in the country, and your start date doesn't care about any of it. The way through is doing things in the right order — because one item unblocks all the others.

Step 0: Housing First (Everything Hangs Off the Address)

Your London address is the keystone: bank account, GP registration, payroll, phone contract — all downstream of it. Which is why the fastest relocations lock housing before the flight, not after. The remote route is well-paved now: live video viewings, online contracts, deposit into a protected scheme — the safe remote-booking playbook — and a managed room means WiFi and bills work from hour one. Expect a 12-month term as the London standard, a four-week deposit (one full rent period), and documents for the Right to Rent check: passport, visa share code if applicable, offer letter. Where to land? The young-professionals map answers by commute.

Week 1: The Paper Cascade

UK bank account. App-based banks (Monzo, Starling, Revolut) approve in a day with passport + UK address — do it before traditional banks ask you for utility bills you don't have yet. Payroll needs this by your first cut-off; find out when that is.

National Insurance number. You can start work without one (employer uses an emergency tax code), but apply in week one — it takes weeks to arrive and overpaid tax is boring to claw back. Apply online; one identity appointment may follow.

GP registration. Free, ten minutes, and everyone defers it until they're ill. Register with the practice near home in week one and never think about it again.

Phone + transport. UK SIM on arrival day (eSIM before you fly is even cleaner); contactless bank card already handles the Tube with weekly fare capping — no setup required, just tap the same card every time.

Month 1: The Settling Layer

Council tax: in an all-inclusive room it's already inside your rent; in a standard flatshare, get your name registered with the borough early (what's usually included where). Emergency fund rebuild: relocation month is the expensive one — the first-quarter budget maps the curve back to normal. And the social layer: if you chose a shared flat, it's built-in; if not, the adult-friendship guide explains why the first months decide the year.

The Relocation Mistakes That Cost Most

Booking a month of Airbnb to "look around". It burns £2,000+ and you still face the same housing decisions, now tired. Decide remotely with live viewings instead. Shipping furniture. Furnished rooms make the container van a £1,500 mistake — the maths. Leaving the NI application for month two. Emergency tax on two paycheques stings.
