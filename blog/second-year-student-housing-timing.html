Second-Year Housing in London: When to Actually Start Looking

The 'all the good houses go by Christmas' myth, debunked with a calendar.

Somewhere around November, every London fresher hears the same warning: "all the good second-year houses go by Christmas." Letting agents say it, older students repeat it, and whole friendship groups sign twelve-month contracts in a panic — with people they've known for eight weeks, for a house they viewed once in the dark. Here's the calendar the warning leaves out.

Where the December Panic Comes From

Partly from agents, whose incentive is early commitment, and partly from a real but misleading observation: the most photogenic student houses do list and sign early. What's false is the scarcity. London is not a small university town with one student street — rooms and whole flats keep listing continuously from January to August, and the September no-show reshuffle releases stock even later. Signing in November doesn't get you a better house; it gets you a longer wait and an eight-week-old friendship underwriting a legal contract.

The Real Calendar

November–December: the pressure window. Fine to research — areas, budgets, who you might live with — but signing now means committing nine months before move-in, with maximum deposit exposure and zero flexibility if friendships shift. (They shift. Second term exists.)

January–March: the sensible window. The market is fully stocked, you've survived one exam season with your prospective flatmates, and you can view in daylight. Most balanced choice of house, price and people.

April–June: still plenty of stock, with landlords getting realistic on price. Late-decider's advantage: you know exactly who you want to live with by now.

July–September: tighter but workable — this is reshuffle season, and managed providers hold year-round availability precisely for it.

The Flatmate Question Matters More Than the Timing

The real second-year risk isn't missing a house — it's signing a joint tenancy with the wrong people. One contract, everyone's name, everyone liable for everyone: if a flatmate drops out, switches unis or falls out with the group, their rent share legally lands on the rest of you. Before any group signing, run the 20 questions honestly, and read what a joint tenancy actually commits you to.

The Route Nobody Mentions at the Freshers Panic

You don't have to assemble a house at all. A managed room — individual 12-month contract, bills inside one weekly price, a four-week deposit (one full rent period) in a protected scheme — removes both the December pressure and the joint-liability gamble. Your flatmates are vetted by the operator, and if the mix doesn't work, moving rooms is an email, not a group legal negotiation. Second year looming? Breathe. Research now, decide in the spring, and see what a managed room looks like before any agent's countdown clock rushes you.
