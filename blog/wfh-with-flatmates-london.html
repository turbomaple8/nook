Working From Home With Flatmates: Making a London Flatshare Work

Two people on calls, one kitchen, one WiFi — the peace treaty, written out.

Hybrid work rewrote flatshare life. The flat that only had to survive evenings and weekends now hosts two Tuesday video calls, a lunchtime gym session in the living room and a fight over who gets the kitchen table. None of this needs drama — it needs about five agreements. Here they are.

1. Your Bedroom Is Your Office — Set It Up Like One

In a shared flat, WFH means working from your room, and the difference between misery and fine is setup: desk positioned for daylight, chair that isn't the bed (your back files complaints by October), headphones as default. If you're room-hunting with a hybrid job, make the desk question part of the viewing — a furnished room with a real desk and strong signal beats an extra square metre. Our viewing checklist covers both.

2. The Common-Area Call Rule

The single biggest WFH flashpoint: calls in shared spaces. The rule that works everywhere: common areas are for camera-off, headphone-in work; anything with a mic live gets a bedroom. Exceptions by request ("big interview at 2, can I take the living room?") are granted freely in flats where the default is respected.

3. WiFi Is Now Infrastructure

Two simultaneous video calls need real bandwidth. Check the speed at peak, not noon; know where the router is relative to your room; and if the flat's connection is part of your rent, treat outages as a maintenance issue to report, not a personal tragedy to tolerate. (In all-inclusive setups, WiFi is the operator's job to fix — one of the quieter arguments for them.)

4. The Heating Conversation, Had Early

A home-all-day flatmate genuinely uses more winter heating than one who's in an office four days a week. In bills-split flats this becomes January resentment unless it's discussed in September: options are a small contribution adjustment, agreed thermostat windows, or the classic jumper-first policy. In all-inclusive flats the cost question vanishes — the bill-splitting maths — though thermostat diplomacy remains a human art.

5. Presence Isn't Availability

The subtlest one: being home all day doesn't make you the flat's receptionist, cleaner or parcel concierge — and your flatmate's closed door at 3pm isn't rudeness, it's a job. Flats that name this early ("closed door = at work, knock like it's an office") skip a whole genre of low-grade friction. End-of-day signals help too: laptop shut, kettle on, human again.

The Meta-Rule

Every item above is ten seconds of slight awkwardness now versus weeks of silent irritation later — the same trade as every flatshare agreement. Raise it in week one while everything's still easy to say; the first-week playbook shows how naturally these fit into settling in.
