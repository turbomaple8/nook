Your First Three Months in London: A Realistic Budget

Month one is expensive, month three is normal — here's the honest curve between them.

Nobody overspends in London because they're careless. They overspend because the first month is structurally expensive, nobody warned them, and the panic-decisions of week two echo for a year. Here's what the first quarter actually looks like on a typical intern or graduate salary — and where the curve bends back to normal.

Month One: The Front-Loaded Month

Assume a £26–34k salary: take-home lands around £1,800–£2,300/month. Month one asks for more than that before your first payday:

Deposit + first rent. The big one. A four-week deposit on a £250/week room is £1,000, plus your first rent period on top — roughly £2,000 leaving your account before you've bought a fork. Whatever you rent, the deposit must sit in a government-approved protection scheme; get the scheme name in writing. Deposits, explained.

Setup costs. Furnished-and-bills-included rooms keep this near zero. Unfurnished routes add £500–£900 of flat-pack and admin — the honest maths — which is exactly the cost you don't want in your most expensive month.

The everything-else of arriving. SIM, adapters, first big shop, one emergency taxi, two "I deserve this" dinners. Budget £200–£300 and forgive yourself.

Month-one total on the low-friction route: roughly £2,300–£2,700. On the DIY-unfurnished route: £3,000+. This is why savings-before-London matter more than salary.

Month Two: The Correction

Payday arrives and the curve bends. Rent settles into its rhythm — one all-inclusive number if you chose that route, or rent-plus-bills-plus-vigilance if not. Transport finds its level: £160–£190 for Zones 1-2, less if you're walking-distance smug. Food lands around £250–£350 depending on how often the office buys lunch.

Month two's trap is social: everyone new to London says yes to everything, and £7 pints compound. The fix isn't hermiting — it's the cheap-London repertoire: markets over restaurants, parks over venues, the flat's kitchen over Deliveroo. If you're in a shared flat, cooking together is both the cheapest and the best evening available — flatmate life, done right.

Month Three: Steady State

By month three you know your number. A realistic London steady state on this salary band: rent-with-bills 40–48% of take-home, transport ~8%, food 12–15%, everything else 20–25%, savings whatever survives — often £100–£250/month at first, and that's fine. If rent-with-bills is eating past 50%, that's the signal to change something at renewal, not to suffer quietly.

Three Rules That Do Most of the Work

1. Compare all-in numbers only. A £230/week bills-included room beats a £205 room plus surprises. Why.

2. Match your contract to your certainty. Three-month flexibility costs little and saves fortunes when rotations move or plans change.

3. Automate the boring 80%. Standing order for rent the day after payday, auto-transfer to savings, and let the remainder be guilt-free.
